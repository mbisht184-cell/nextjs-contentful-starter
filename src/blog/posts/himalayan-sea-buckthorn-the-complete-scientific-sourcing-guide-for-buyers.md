\## What is Himalayan Sea Buckthorn?



Sea buckthorn (\*Hippophae rhamnoides\* L., family Elaeagnaceae) is a cold-desert shrub native to the Trans-Himalayan belt, growing wild at 2,500-4,300 metres across Ladakh, Lahaul-Spiti, Kinnaur, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, and Arunachal Pradesh. It survives temperature swings from -43C to +40C, and that stress is precisely what makes it valuable: the plant responds by producing an unusually dense concentration of bioactive compounds - close to 200 identified molecules across its fruit, seed, and leaf.



Sea buckthorn carries India's Geographical Indication (GI) tag, awarded in 2023, and is regulated under FSSAI, codified in the Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India, and has been studied by DRDO, CSIR, and ICMR.



\## Why Its Chemistry Is Unusual



Sea buckthorn is one of very few plants on earth carrying Omega-3, 6, 7, and 9 fatty acids simultaneously - but not in one uniform mix. Pulp/fruit oil is dominated by Omega-7 (palmitoleic acid, >36% in authentic product), used for skin, mucosal and wound-healing applications. Seed oil carries a near 1:1 Omega-3 to Omega-6 ratio, relevant for cardiovascular and anti-inflammatory formulations - a genuine differentiator since most modern diets are Omega-6 dominant, linked to pro-inflammatory outcomes.



Berries also carry up to 909 mg of vitamin C per 100g - roughly 10-15x the vitamin C content of oranges - along with a dense phytosterol profile (beta-sitosterol, campesterol) at 2-4x the concentration found in soybean oil, and all 8 essential amino acids.



\## Clinical Evidence



Unlike many superfood botanicals, sea buckthorn has registered human clinical trials on India's CTRI (Clinical Trials Registry - India):



\- CTRI/2015/11/006368 - Daily oral seed oil supplementation in 74 hypertensive subjects produced a mean systolic blood pressure reduction of 9.57 mmHg and diastolic reduction of 4.96 mmHg over 30 days, alongside reduced total cholesterol and oxidized LDL.

\- CTRI/2019/05/01911 - A non-hormonal vaginal gel using the Omega-7 fraction showed statistically significant improvement (P < .0001) in vulvovaginal atrophy symptoms in postmenopausal women.

\- DRDO/DIHAR research validated sea buckthorn as a prophylactic against High-Altitude Pulmonary Edema (HAPE) and cognitive decline under hypoxia, leading to its approval for use by Indian armed forces personnel at altitude.



Toxicology studies found no mortality at 20 mL/kg acute dosing and no organ pathology at a 10 mL/kg No-Observed-Adverse-Effect Level (NOAEL), supporting a strong long-term safety profile for prophylactic use in healthy adults.



\## Who Should Avoid It



\- Pregnant women (no first-trimester human safety data)

\- Patients on anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy (Warfarin, Heparin, Aspirin)

\- Patients scheduled for surgery (discontinue at least 2 weeks prior)

\- Individuals with hypotension or already on anti-hypertensive medication

\- Children under 2 (restricted under FSSAI Schedule IV)

\- Patients with active gallstones or biliary obstruction



\## The Adulteration Problem - and How to Verify Authenticity



A known issue in the sea buckthorn trade: authentic fruit pulp oil (dominated by palmitoleic acid, >36%) is frequently diluted with cheaper seed oil (linolenic acid >54%) and mislabeled. CSIR-IHBT lab testing using qNMR and GC-MS has confirmed this adulteration is widespread in the open market.



What buyers should demand: a verified Certificate of Analysis (CoA) from an accredited lab for every batch, specifying the fatty acid profile relevant to the labeled fraction (pulp vs. seed).
